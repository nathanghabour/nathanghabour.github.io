The Challenge

Enterprise-scale ML validation for a novel health feature

When Amazon acquired Body Labs, the mandate was clear: take the world's most accurate statistical body model and put it to work inside Amazon's product ecosystem. The first major application was body composition analysis for the Amazon Halo health band — estimating body fat percentage from smartphone photos, with clinical-grade accuracy.

This required validating computer vision algorithms at a scale and rigor that Amazon's compliance standards demanded. Collecting body photography at scale is not a simple problem: it touches privacy law, research ethics, data security, and participant consent in ways that most product teams never encounter.

ML Validation: Accuracy at Clinical Scale

  • Designed and ran the validation study comparing Halo body composition to clinical gold standards
  • Established the accuracy benchmarks that justified shipping the feature
  • Achieved 2× the accuracy of smart scales — the leading consumer body composition alternative

Compliance: Collecting Body Photos at Scale

  • Navigated the regulatory and ethical requirements for collecting body photography from research participants
  • Built consent frameworks, data handling protocols, and IRB-aligned study designs
  • Established the data capture infrastructure that enabled all subsequent Amazon Digital Health body features
  • Turned compliance from a blocker into a repeatable playbook
🔒

Privacy-first data collection framework — IRB-aligned, consent-driven, scalable across all Amazon Digital Health body features

Key insight: Collecting body data at enterprise scale is not a technical problem — it's a trust problem. The frameworks we built for consent, security, and compliance weren't bureaucratic overhead; they were the foundation that made the data worth having. Good data infrastructure is built on participant trust first, algorithms second.

Amazon Halo launched with body composition analysis as a flagship feature — validated by the accuracy studies I led. The data capture infrastructure built during this period became the foundation for all Digital Health body features that followed. The compliance playbook made it repeatable.

Why It Matters

Accuracy claims require accuracy proof

In consumer health, the gap between "this works in the lab" and "this works for every user" is enormous. The validation framework built here — study design, compliance protocol, accuracy benchmarking — is the template for any team building body data features for real-world use.

At Nike, I applied this same discipline to a 1.1M body scan database: rigorous data collection, clear provenance, defensible methodology. Whether you're shipping to millions of Amazon customers or informing a $50B sizing system, the principle is the same — you can only trust the output as much as you trust the input.
