The Challenge

Academic breakthrough, no clear path to market

Body Labs had world-class research: SMPL (Skinned Multi-Person Linear model) was the most accurate statistical body model ever built, capable of representing virtually any human shape and pose. But research and product are different problems. The technology lived in academic papers and research demos — not in anything a business could buy or a developer could build on.

The challenge: as the sole product manager, define what this technology could actually do, who needed it, and build the roadmap to get it into their hands — across two distinct markets and eight distinct capabilities.

Defining the Capability Set

SMPL could do far more than any single vertical would require. The first task was inventorying what the model was actually capable of and translating research outputs into product language buyers could understand.

  • Pose — place a virtual body into any anatomical position
  • Animate — drive realistic body motion sequences
  • Measure — extract precise body measurements from 3D shape
  • Compare — diff any two body shapes or poses
  • Average — compute population-level body shape statistics
  • Analyze — query biomechanical properties of a given shape
  • Predict — infer body shape from sparse inputs (photos, measurements)
  • Design — generate products fit to a specific body or population

Evolving Input Modalities

The underlying challenge for all markets was the same: how do you capture a person's body at scale? The input technology was evolving rapidly, and the product roadmap had to evolve with it.

  • Built product strategy across three generations of capture technology
  • Laser scanners — high accuracy, specialized hardware, limited deployment
  • Smartphone cameras — accessible, but required photogrammetry pipelines
  • CNN-based inference — predict shape from a single photo; the path to scale
  • Each modality unlocked different markets and use cases

Key insight: The same underlying model — SMPL — was genuinely applicable across wildly different use cases. The product work was not building different models for different markets; it was building the right API surface and tooling so each market could consume the same capability in the way that made sense for them.

The Outcome

Acquired by Amazon.

Body Labs was acquired by Amazon — a validation that the body model technology was strategically valuable at platform scale. The SMPL model and the product infrastructure built around it became the foundation for Amazon's body-related product development, including the Amazon Halo body composition features that followed.

Why It Matters

The body model as infrastructure

Body Labs proved that a statistical model of the human form could be a platform — not just a research artifact. The same underlying representation could power fashion, gaming, healthcare, and ergonomics. What changed between markets wasn't the model; it was the interface.

This is the pattern I've carried forward: build the canonical data layer once, then design interfaces that let different stakeholders consume it in ways that fit their workflows. At Nike, a 1.1M-scan database became that same kind of infrastructure — one source of truth that a scientist, a designer, and a business director could all query differently.
